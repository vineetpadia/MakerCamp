My Highlights from Shenzhen 2025

My recent trip to Shenzhen was nothing short of an adventure. Here's what caught my attention during this visit.

Scalable HCI Research Symposium

I presented our latest work on compliant twisted and winching tendon actuators for lightweight exoskeletons at the Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) symposium. One highlight was Eric Pan from Seeed Studio emphasizing the importance of aligning product quality with customer expectations. His insights on continuously adapting products based on customer feedback resonated deeply.

Art + Robots at AIRS

At the Shenzhen Institute of Artificial Intelligence and Robotics for Society (AIRS), I was fascinated by the robotics showcase featuring climbing robots. These machines skillfully combined aesthetics with functional engineering—prompting me to connect my MIT colleagues with these innovations. Nearby, an interesting logistics station featured drones collaborating with human workers for package delivery.

Inside the Incubator

Exploring the tech incubator offered a captivating glimpse of ideas transitioning from prototypes to market-ready products. Among the notable innovations was an electronic guitar rapidly gaining popularity across Shenzhen—my friend Samy enthusiastically tested one out. The incubator's creative vibe, enhanced by a cat café, transformed the space from sterile to genuinely inspiring.

Factory Visits

I toured various electronics manufacturing factories, witnessing advanced pick-and-place PCB assembly, precision injection mold-making processes, and massive machinery far surpassing what we have on campus. As a tool enthusiast, I was intrigued by unfamiliar but impressive machinery brands. At a rapid prototyping facility reminiscent of Protolabs, I observed extensive carbon fiber layups. Other highlights included factories producing servo motors (like those found in RC cars), microLED displays, and luxury smart wearables featuring genuine gold components.

HQB: Tool Paradise Found

For a tool aficionado, Huaqiangbei (HQB) was paradise. I acquired specialized electronics tools, including precision tweezers I’ve wanted for months. Alan humorously posed with a giant screwdriver, and I spent significant time admiring a creatively decorated microscope—prompting thoughts about upgrading my lab’s soldering equipment back at MIT.

Magnetic Circuits: Project in Progress

I’ve been developing reconfigurable electronics inspired by Lectron kits but optimized for accessibility and educational use. Current prototypes use 6mm magnetic connectors; however, I aim to shrink them further to about 15mm per module. An open-source version could significantly enhance electronics education by providing clear, intuitive insight into circuit assembly.

![[IMG_5672.jpg]]

Soft Robotics Ideas

My colleague Ryan and I recently developed a self-sensing tendon actuator (preprint available, full paper forthcoming). We discussed how soft robotics demonstrations often conceal bulky power and control valves. At HQB, I found exceptionally compact valves (under 9mm), ideal for discreetly powering soft robotics, particularly in lab automation scenarios such as standard 96-well plate experiments.

"YOU ARE A CHICKEN": Hackathon Project on Digital Conditioning

At a hackathon, I created a digital conditioning project humorously titled "YOU ARE A CHICKEN," exploring parallels between human and animal behavior through technology. A sensor activated by a chicken (named Snowball) triggered a screen displaying an endless, social-media-like feed and dispensed seed rewards via a motor. This project highlighted the similarities between animal conditioning and human digital behaviors, emphasizing our susceptibility to endless information streams.

HQB Mapping Project: Coming Soon

I’m planning a data-driven project to map Huaqiangbei (HQB) using 3D scanning and analysis, aiming to help global makers better navigate this complex marketplace. With detailed navigation data, this initiative could significantly support makers and entrepreneurs in sourcing tools and components.

From practical tools to philosophical chickens, Shenzhen has once again sparked my curiosity and filled my notebook with exciting ideas to explore.
